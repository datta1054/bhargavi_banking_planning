### Memory, Retention, and Peak Performance

---

## 10.1 Why Revision is as Important as Learning

The human brain forgets approximately:
- 50% of new information within 1 hour of learning
- 70% within 24 hours
- 80% within 1 week

This is called the **Forgetting Curve** (Ebbinghaus). The solution is **spaced repetition** — revisiting information at increasing intervals:

```
SPACED REPETITION SCHEDULE FOR ANY NEW TOPIC:

Day 0   → Learn it
Day 1   → First revision (10–15 min)
Day 3   → Second revision (5–10 min)
Day 7   → Third revision (5 min)
Day 14  → Fourth revision (5 min)
Day 30  → Fifth revision (2–3 min)

After 5 revisions → Long-term memory
```

**Application in your plan:** Every Saturday's weekly revision is built around this system. Subjects studied on Monday are revised Saturday. Topics from 2 weeks ago get a brief revisit. This is why the weekly review day is non-negotiable.

## 10.2 Subject-Specific Revision Strategies

### Quantitative Aptitude Revision

**What to revise and how often:**

| Topic Type | Revision Frequency | Method |
|-----------|-------------------|--------|
| Formulas (all key formulas) | Weekly | Write formula from memory, check |
| Fraction-% conversions | Daily (first 2 months) | Flashcards or mental recall |
| Solved examples (1 per topic) | Every 2 weeks | Redo from scratch |
| Weak topics (from mock analysis) | 2–3 times/week | Practice questions |
| DI sets | 3–4 sets/week (Phase 2 onwards) | Timed practice |

**QA Revision Notebook Contents:**
1. All formulas organized topic-wise
2. Shortcut methods for each topic
3. 3 typical examples per topic with solution
4. Common mistake warnings

### Reasoning Revision

**What to revise:**
- Puzzle-solving approach (not formulas — method and mindset)
- Syllogism shortcut rules
- Inequality rules

**Method:**
- Revisit one puzzle set per week from earlier practice material
- The goal is to see if you can still solve it quickly
- Re-reading solved puzzles is NOT enough — must attempt fresh

### English Revision

**Grammar rules:** Write a rule-by-rule summary sheet (1–2 pages). Read it once every week.

**Vocabulary:** Review your vocabulary notebook every week — 5 minutes, scan all words.

**Reading:** Consistent daily reading (15 min/day) IS the revision — no separate revision needed.

### GA/Banking Revision

**Static GK:** 
- Create a "condensed fact sheet" — one page with 100 key facts
- Review this sheet every 2 weeks

**Banking Awareness:**
- Maintain a banking terms glossary (handwritten, 2–3 pages)
- Review once every 10 days

**Current Affairs:**
- Daily reading = primary learning
- Monthly CA quiz from an exam platform = revision
- End-of-quarter (every 3 months): Full recap of last 3 months' CA

## 10.5 Exam-Day Strategy

### The Night Before the Exam

| Time | Activity |
|------|---------|
| 6:00 PM | Finish studying — No new material after this |
| 7:00 PM | Lay out everything needed: Admit card (printed × 2), ID proof, stationery, water bottle |
| 8:00 PM | Light dinner |
| 9:00 PM | Review ONLY your summary/formula sheet (20 minutes) |
| 9:30 PM | No phone/social media. Light reading or music. |
| 10:00 PM | Sleep — aim for 7–8 hours |

### Morning of the Exam

| Time | Activity |
|------|---------|
| 5:30–6:00 AM | Wake up (for a 9 AM exam) |
| 6:00–6:30 AM | Light breakfast — NOT heavy food (affects alertness) |
| 6:30 AM | Read your formula sheet — ONE final time (15 min only) |
| 7:00 AM | Leave for exam center (plan to arrive 45+ min early) |
| 8:00 AM | Arrive at exam center, find seat, relax |
| 8:30 AM | Light mental warm-up: Do 5 simple mental math questions in your head |

### Inside the Exam Hall

**Before starting:**
- Check the screen — verify subject, number of questions, time per section
- Read instructions if any
- Take 3 slow breaths

**During the exam:**
1. **Start with your strongest section** (to build confidence and secure early marks)
2. **Do NOT attempt in serial order** — use the 3-round strategy (see [11_Practice_Mock_Strategy.md#11.7](11_Practice_Mock_Strategy.md#11.7))
3. **Watch the time every 5 minutes** — glance at timer, stay aware
4. **If stuck on a question for 2+ minutes:** SKIP — move on. Return only if time permits.
5. **Never leave a section incomplete** due to obsessing over hard questions
6. **With 5 minutes remaining:** Review skipped questions, make educated guesses only where you've eliminated 2 options

**Section-specific timing reminders:**
- 20 min for QA: If at 15 min and have 8+ questions left, speed up
- 20 min for Reasoning: Puzzles eat time — attempt only 2 puzzle sets in Prelims
- 20 min for English: RC should start only if 8+ min remain

### What to Do If You Feel Panic

> "I don't know this question" → **Normal.** Skip it. There will be questions you don't know.  
> "Time is running out" → **Take one deep breath.** Attempt only questions you're confident about.  
> "Everyone seems to be typing faster than me" → **Ignore others.** Focus on your screen only.  
> "I'm making silly mistakes" → **Slow down 10%.** Accuracy beats speed with negative marking.

## 10.6 Post-Exam Strategy

### If Prelims Result is Not What You Expected

Banking exam results take 4–6 weeks. During this wait:
- **Do not stop studying** — other exams are coming
- Continue mock tests (2/week)
- Update current affairs
- Apply for other banking exams with open notifications

### If You Clear Prelims

Immediately shift to:
- Mains preparation mode (see [07_Monthly_Plan.md](07_Monthly_Plan.md) — Month 7–8)
- More complex DI, Data Analysis, Computer Aptitude
- Descriptive writing (essay + letter) practice
- Banking awareness depth increase
- Interview preparation intensification

## 10.7 Resource Management for Revision

### What to Keep and What to Skip

**Keep for Revision:**
- Your personal error notebook ✅
- Summary/formula sheets ✅
- GA vocabulary notebook ✅
- Banking awareness glossary ✅
- Weekly revision notes ✅

**Do NOT spend revision time on:**
- Re-reading entire chapters/books ❌
- Watching long YouTube videos on topics you already know ❌
- Solving new types of questions you've never practiced ❌ (last 30 days — no new topics)
- Comparing notes with friends (creates doubt and anxiety) ❌
