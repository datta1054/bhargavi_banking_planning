## Section A: Quantitative Aptitude / Numerical Ability

### Strategic Overview

QA is the **highest-anxiety section** for most students, but it is also one of the most **mechanical** — meaning it can be mastered with consistent practice. Unlike English, which requires subjective comprehension, QA has definitive right and wrong answers and follows learnable patterns.

**Your B.Com advantage:** You've already studied basic mathematics. Even if you've forgotten it, the concepts will come back faster than for someone without this background.

### QA Learning Strategy — Topic by Topic

#### Simplification & Approximation (Week 1–2)
- Learn BODMAS rule thoroughly with examples
- Practice mental calculation: squares up to 30, cubes up to 20, tables up to 20
- Learn fraction ↔ percentage conversions (1/4 = 25%, 1/3 = 33.33%, etc.)
- **Daily Practice:** 20 simplification questions — aim for 90%+ accuracy

#### Number Series (Week 3)
Types to master:
1. **Arithmetic progressions** (+3, +6, +9...)
2. **Geometric progressions** (×2, ×3...)
3. **Square/Cube series** (1, 4, 9, 16...)
4. **Mixed operations** (+2, ×2, +2, ×2...)
5. **Wrong number series** (find the odd one out)
- Practice minimum 30 series questions per day during this week

#### Percentage (Week 3–4)
- Understand percentage as "per hundred"
- Learn fraction-percentage equivalents by heart
- Application: Percentage increase/decrease, successive changes
- **Key formula:** Successive % change = A + B + AB/100
- Practice 15–20 problems per session

#### Ratio, Proportion & Average (Week 5)
- Ratio as comparison — understand before formula
- Proportion: Direct vs. Inverse
- Average: Sum ÷ Count — also weighted average
- Relation between Average, Sum, and Number of terms

#### Profit & Loss (Week 6)
- CP, SP, Profit%, Loss% — clear concept first
- Marked Price, Discount, Successive discounts
- Dishonest shopkeeper problems
- **B.Com connection:** You know these from your accountancy studies!

#### Simple & Compound Interest (Week 7)
- SI formula: P × R × T / 100
- CI formula: P(1 + R/100)^n
- Difference between SI and CI for 2 and 3 years (learn by formula)
- Half-yearly/quarterly compounding

#### Time, Speed & Distance + Time & Work (Week 8–9)
- TSD: Distance = Speed × Time
- Average speed formula (harmonic mean when same distance)
- Trains: Length considerations
- Time & Work: Work = Rate × Time, LCM method
- Pipes: Filling vs. emptying, combined work

#### Data Interpretation (Phase 2 — Weeks 12–16)
DI is the most important topic for IBPS Mains. It appears as sets of 5 questions each.

**Types to Master:**

| DI Type | Difficulty | Frequency | Time per Set |
|---------|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| Table DI | Low | ★★★★★ | 6–7 min |
| Bar Graph | Low-Med | ★★★★★ | 5–6 min |
| Line Graph | Med | ★★★★☆ | 6–7 min |
| Pie Chart | Med | ★★★★☆ | 7–8 min |
| Missing Data DI | High | ★★★☆☆ | 8–10 min |
| Caselet DI | Very High | ★★★☆☆ | 10–12 min |

**DI Practice Approach:**
1. Week 12: Table DI only (5 sets/day)
2. Week 13: Bar + Line graphs (4 sets/day mixed)
3. Week 14: Pie Charts (4 sets/day)
4. Week 15: Mixed DI + Missing Data
5. Week 16: Caselet DI

## Section B: Reasoning Ability

### Strategic Overview

Reasoning is the **most time-sensitive section** in banking exams. It contains long-format puzzles that can eat 12–15 minutes if approached wrong. The key to Reasoning is:
1. Recognizing puzzle types instantly
2. Having a systematic approach for each type
3. Knowing when to skip and move on

### Reasoning Learning Strategy — Key Topics

#### Syllogisms (Week 1)
Syllogisms are 3–5 questions answered in 2–3 minutes if you know the rules.

**Shortcut Rules to Memorize:**

| Statement Combination | Valid Conclusion |
|----------------------|-----------------|
| All A → B | Some B → A (valid) |
| All A → B + All B → C | All A → C (valid) |
| Some A → B + All B → C | Some A → C (valid) |
| No A → B | No B → A (valid) |
| Some A → B + No B → C | Some A → not C (valid) |

**Method:** Learn Venn diagram method first (weeks 1–2), then transition to shortcut rules.

#### Coding-Decoding Patterns to Master
1. **Letter shifting:** A=1, B=2... or A=Z, B=Y (reversed)
2. **Position-based:** Opposite letters (A↔Z, B↔Y)
3. **Fictitious language:** Direct word-to-word mapping
4. **Number coding:** Letter = its position number

#### Seating Arrangement — Make-or-Break Topic

**Linear Arrangement (Phase 1, Week 8):**
- Single row facing North/South
- Double row (facing each other)
- **Practice method:** Draw a straight line, place people one by one

**Circular Arrangement (Phase 1, Week 9):**
- All facing center (most common)
- All facing outward
- Mixed facing
- **Key rule:** Person's LEFT = clockwise direction when facing center

#### Puzzles — Highest Weightage Topic (Phase 2)

**Floor Puzzle Method:**
```
6th Floor: ?
5th Floor: ?          → Read all clues
4th Floor: ?          → Place definite (absolute) clues first
3rd Floor: ?          → Use elimination for the rest
2nd Floor: ?
1st Floor: ?
```

**Puzzle Mastery Sequence:**
- Floor Puzzle (5-person, 1 attribute) → Floor Puzzle (2 attributes) → Box Puzzle → Month/Year Puzzle → Complex Mixed Puzzles

### Reasoning Topic Dependency Flow


## Section C: English Language

### Strategic Overview — From Fundamentals

English is your weakest area. The good news: 9 months is **more than enough** time to reach banking exam English proficiency. The exam tests grammar, comprehension, and vocabulary in a specific, predictable way — NOT advanced literary English.

**The English Improvement Formula:**
```
Daily Reading (15 min) + Grammar Study (30 min) + 
Vocabulary (10 min) + Question Practice (20 min) 
= Consistent English Improvement
```

### English Learning Timeline

| Month | Focus | Activity |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Aug–Sep | Grammar Basics | Parts of Speech, Tenses, SVA |
| Oct | Intermediate Grammar | Active/Passive, Direct/Indirect, Prepositions |
| Nov | Application | Error Detection, Fill in Blanks |
| Dec | Reading | Reading Comprehension (easy to medium) |
| Jan | Exam Patterns | Cloze Test, Para Jumbles, Phrase Replacement |
| Feb | Advanced RC | Long passages, inference questions |
| Mar–Apr | Speed + Writing | Faster RC, Essay/Letter for PO |
| May | Revision | PYQ English sets |

### Grammar Rules Must-Know List (Priority Order)

**Level 1 — Must Know (Phase 1)**
1. Subject-Verb Agreement (singular/plural rules)
2. Tense consistency rules
3. Article usage (A, An, The — 15 key rules)
4. Preposition usage (at/in/on for time and place)
5. Pronoun agreement (it/they/him/her matches noun)
6. Comparison degrees (good/better/best)

**Level 2 — Should Know (Phase 1–2)**
7. Active vs. Passive Voice transformation
8. Direct vs. Indirect speech rules
9. Conjunctions (and, but, either/or, neither/nor)
10. Conditional sentences (If clauses — Type 1, 2, 3)
11. Relative pronouns (who/which/that — when to use each)
12. Gerund vs. Infinitive

**Level 3 — Exam Edge (Phase 2)**
13. Redundancy errors ("She entered *inside*" ❌ → "She entered" ✅)
14. Wrong verb form errors
15. Dangling modifiers
16. Parallelism errors

### Reading Comprehension Strategy

**Phase 1 Reading (Easy):**
- Read simple English articles: Inshorts App, student-level editorials
- Focus: Understanding what the paragraph is about

**Phase 2 Reading (Medium):**
- The Hindu editorial / Financial Express
- One editorial every day (500–800 words)
- Focus: Main idea, tone, inference

**Phase 3 Reading (Exam-level):**
- Practice RC sets from IBPS PYQ papers (timed)
- Target: 500-word passage in under 8 minutes
- Focus: Speed without losing accuracy

**RC Question Types to Master:**
1. Main idea / Central theme
2. Author's opinion / Tone
3. Vocabulary in context
4. Inference (not directly stated)
5. Specific detail
6. Title of the passage

> **Pro Strategy:** Read the questions FIRST, then read the passage. This way you know exactly what to look for while reading.

### Vocabulary Building System

**The 5-Word Daily System:**
- Every day: Learn 5 new words
- Method: Learn in a sentence (not just the dictionary meaning)
- Notebook format: `Word | Meaning | Synonyms | Example Sentence`
- Review previous week's words every Sunday (5 minutes)

**Banking Vocabulary — Learn First:**
- Fiscal, Monetary, Liquidity, Inflation, Deflation
- Repo, Reverse Repo, CRR, SLR, Marginal Standing Facility
- Collateral, Mortgaged, Annexed, Appropriated
- Prudent, Stringent, Robust, Voluminous, Persistent

**General Exam Vocabulary:**
- Focus on words that appear frequently in banking news and editorials
- Learn antonyms alongside synonyms (Synonyms/Antonyms questions appear in exams)

### Writing Practice — IBPS PO Mains (Phase 3 Onwards)

**Essay Topics to Practice:**
- Digital Banking and Financial Inclusion
- Non-Performing Assets (NPAs) in Indian Banks
- Role of RBI in the Indian Economy
- Cryptocurrency and its regulation
- Women Empowerment and Banking Access
- Green Banking / Sustainable Finance
- MSME Lending Challenges

**Letter Writing Types:**
- Formal letter to bank manager (complaint, request)
- Letter to RBI regarding banking issues
- Business communication letters

**Format:**
- Introduction (3–4 lines) → Body (3–4 paragraphs) → Conclusion (2–3 lines)
- Word count: 200–250 words for both essays and letters
- **Avoid:** Informal language, slang, contractions (don't → do not)
