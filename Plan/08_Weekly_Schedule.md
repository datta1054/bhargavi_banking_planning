# 08 · Weekly Study Schedule
### Weekly Timetables & Structured Study Templates

---

## 8.1 Why Structure Matters More Than Duration

The most common preparation mistake is studying randomly — picking topics based on mood, spending too long on favorite subjects, and neglecting weaker ones. A structured schedule ensures:
- **Every subject gets attention every week** (no subject is neglected)
- **Topics are covered in the right sequence** (no jumping ahead)
- **Revision happens before you forget** (not just at exam time)
- **Energy is used efficiently** (hardest subjects when you're freshest)

For a detailed daily breakdown of study hours, see [09_Daily_Study_Allocation.md](09_Daily_Study_Allocation.md).

## 8.6 B.Com College + Banking Exam Balance

Since you are in your final year of B.Com, your schedule must adapt to your academic calendar:
- **Regular college days:** Keep banking study to a strict 2–2.5 hours. Do not try to stretch.
- **Exam periods (internal/semester):** Reduce banking prep to 1–1.5 hours of light study (mainly GA & vocab) but **never stop completely**.
- **Holidays/vacations:** Use these as bonus days to study 4–5 hours.
- **Semester exam months (Nov–Dec 2026 & Mar–Apr 2027):** Run the reduced exam-week schedules.

### College Exam Period Strategy
- **2 weeks before exams:** Cut banking study to 1 hour/day (only GA and vocab review).
- **During exam week:** 30 minutes of daily GA reading only. This ensures you keep the study habit active and don't lose GA momentum.
- **Post-exams:** Resume your full weekly schedule within 2 days.

## 8.7 Saturday Weekly Review System

Every Saturday, complete this 4-step review:

1. **Topic Review (20 min):** Identify what was covered this week, what was understood, and what needs more practice.
2. **Mistake Review (20 min):** Review 10 questions from your error notebook and verify you understand the correct method.
3. **Weekly Mini Test (30 min):** Take a small quiz containing 15 QA, 15 Reasoning, and 10 English questions from the week's topics.
4. **Next Week Planning (10 min):** Check upcoming topics in the monthly plan and check availability of materials.

## 8.8 Subject Rotation Logic

The daily subject order (**QA → Reasoning → English → GA**) is designed based on cognitive energy levels:

| Order | Subject | Reason |
|-------|---------|--------|
| **1st** | Quantitative Aptitude | Highest cognitive load; must be done when your mind is freshest. |
| **2nd** | Reasoning Ability | High logical workload; patterns are easier to spot after math warm-up. |
| **3rd** | English Language | Verbal processing; can be done with moderate focus. |
| **4th** | GA/Banking | Reading and memory; effective even at lower energy levels. |

> **Crucial Tip:** Do not study GA first just because it feels "easier". It will exhaust your reading stamina and leave you with less energy for math and logic.
