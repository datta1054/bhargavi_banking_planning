This 20-mark difference from GA alone can be the gap between clearing and failing the Mains cutoff. **GA is the section where consistent daily effort pays off the most.**

**Good news:** GA is purely memory-based. No calculations, no complex reasoning. Just systematic reading and revision.

### Phase 1 Banking Awareness (Aug–Oct) — The Foundations

**History of Banking in India**

```
1770 → Bank of Hindustan (first bank in India, Calcutta)
1806 → Bank of Bengal (presidency bank)
1840 → Bank of Bombay
1843 → Bank of Madras
1921 → Imperial Bank of India (merger of 3 presidency banks)
1935 → Reserve Bank of India established (April 1, 1935)
       → RBI nationalized: January 1, 1949
1955 → Imperial Bank becomes State Bank of India (SBI Act, 1955)
1969 → Nationalization of 14 major private banks (under Indira Gandhi)
1980 → Nationalization of 6 more private banks
1991 → Banking sector liberalization (Narasimham Committee)
1993 → New private banks licensed (ICICI, HDFC, Axis, etc.)
2015 → Payments Banks and Small Finance Banks licensed
2020 → Merger of 10 PSBs into 4 (SBI, PNB, Union Bank, Canara Bank as anchor banks)
```

### Phase 2 Banking Awareness (Nov–Jan) — Structure & Policy

**Reserve Bank of India (RBI) — Complete Overview**

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Established | April 1, 1935 |
| Nationalized | January 1, 1949 |
| Headquarters | Mumbai |
| Current Governor | (Check latest — changes with appointments) |
| Deputy Governors | 4 Deputy Governors |
| Offices | 31 offices/branches across India |

**Functions of RBI:**
1. **Monopoly of Note Issue:** Only RBI can print currency notes (except ₹1 coin, minted by Govt.)
2. **Banker to Government:** Manages government accounts and borrowings
3. **Banker to Banks:** Provides banking services to commercial banks
4. **Lender of Last Resort:** Provides emergency loans to banks in crisis
5. **Manager of Foreign Exchange:** Maintains forex reserves, manages exchange rate
6. **Monetary Policy:** Controls money supply and interest rates
7. **Regulatory Authority:** Supervises and regulates all commercial banks
8. **Payment & Settlement Systems:** Oversees RTGS, NEFT, UPI systems
9. **Credit Control:** Ensures credit flows to productive sectors

**Monetary Policy Tools — The Most Asked Topic**

| Tool | Current Rate | What it Controls |
|------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Repo Rate** | Check RBI website | Rate at which RBI lends to commercial banks (overnight, against securities). High repo = expensive borrowing = less money in economy = controls inflation |
| **Reverse Repo Rate** | Repo rate – 0.25% | Rate at which RBI borrows from commercial banks. Banks park surplus money here |
| **CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio)** | 4% (check latest) | % of Net Demand & Time Liabilities (NDTL) banks must keep as cash with RBI. Not lendable. High CRR = less money to lend = tight credit |
| **SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio)** | 18% (check latest) | % of NDTL banks must maintain as liquid assets (gold, govt. securities). High SLR = less funds for lending |
| **MSF (Marginal Standing Facility)** | Repo rate + 0.25% | Emergency overnight borrowing by banks from RBI, above repo rate |
| **Bank Rate** | MSF rate | Rate at which RBI rediscounts bills — signals long-term policy stance |
| **OMO (Open Market Operations)** | Variable | RBI buys/sells government securities to inject/absorb liquidity |
| **LAF (Liquidity Adjustment Facility)** | Via Repo/Rev Repo | RBI's toolkit for day-to-day liquidity management |

> ⚠️ **Always check the CURRENT rates** before an exam. Rates change every 2 months (RBI MPC meets bi-monthly). Last checked: August 2026.

**Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)**
- Established under RBI Act 1934 (amendment 2016)
- 6 members: 3 from RBI + 3 external members appointed by Government
- Meets every 2 months (6 times a year) to set the repo rate
- Decisions by majority vote; RBI Governor has casting vote

**Banking Regulation Act, 1949**
- Primary legislation governing commercial banks in India
- Powers: RBI can inspect, audit, and regulate banks under this act
- Covers: Licensing, branch opening, capital adequacy, loans, investments

**NABARD (National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development)**
- Established: July 12, 1982 (based on B. Sivaraman Committee)
- Headquarters: Mumbai
- Functions: Refinancing rural credit, promoting rural development, supervising RRBs and cooperative banks

**SIDBI (Small Industries Development Bank of India)**
- Established: April 2, 1990
- Headquarters: Lucknow
- Functions: Financing MSMEs, promoting entrepreneurship, refinancing banks that lend to small industries

**NHB (National Housing Bank)**
- Established: 1988 (under NHB Act, 1987)
- Headquarters: New Delhi
- Functions: Regulates housing finance companies, refinances housing loans

**EXIM Bank**
- Full form: Export-Import Bank of India
- Established: 1982
- Headquarters: Mumbai
- Functions: Financing international trade, export credit, project exports

**Payment Systems**

| System | Full Form | Settlement | Limit | Timing |
|--------|-----------|-----------|-------|--------|
| NEFT | National Electronic Funds Transfer | Net settlement (batch) | No upper limit | 24×7 (from Dec 2019) |
| RTGS | Real Time Gross Settlement | Immediate, one-by-one | Min ₹2 lakh | 24×7 (from Dec 2020) |
| IMPS | Immediate Payment Service | Immediate | Up to ₹5 lakh | 24×7 |
| UPI | Unified Payments Interface | Immediate | Up to ₹1 lakh (₹5L for verified) | 24×7 |
| NACH | National Automated Clearing House | Batch | Used for recurring payments | Business days |
| Cheque | — | 2 days (CTS) | No limit | Business days |

**Types of Cheques (Exam Frequent!)**

| Cheque Type | Definition |
|------------|-----------|
| Bearer Cheque | Payable to whoever presents it |
| Order Cheque | Payable only to named person |
| Crossed Cheque | Two parallel lines across — must be deposited, not encashed |
| Account Payee Cheque | Stricter crossing — must be credited to named account only |
| Post-dated Cheque | Date in future — cannot be encashed before that date |
| Stale Cheque | Presented after 3 months of issue date — invalid |
| Ante-dated Cheque | Date in the past |
| Blank Cheque | Amount not filled — risky! |

**NPA (Non-Performing Asset)**
- A loan/advance where interest or principal repayment is overdue for **more than 90 days**
- **Classification:**
  - Sub-standard: NPA for less than or equal to 12 months
  - Doubtful: NPA for more than 12 months
  - Loss: NPA identified as uncollectable by bank/auditor/RBI
- **Gross NPA %:** Total NPAs / Total Loans × 100
- **Net NPA %:** (Gross NPA − Provisions) / (Total Loans − Provisions) × 100
- **SARFAESI Act (2002):** Banks can recover loans by selling pledged assets WITHOUT court order
- **IBC (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016):** Resolves bad loans through National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) within 270 days

**Basel Norms (Bank for International Settlements, Basel, Switzerland)**
- International standards for bank capital adequacy, stress testing, and liquidity
- **Basel I (1988):** First framework — minimum 8% Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR)
- **Basel II (2004):** Added operational risk and market risk pillars
- **Basel III (2010, post 2008 crisis):** Stricter capital requirements, added liquidity ratios
  - Minimum CAR: 10.5% (including capital conservation buffer)
  - Tier 1 Capital: Core capital (equity + retained earnings)
  - Tier 2 Capital: Supplementary capital (provisions, subordinated debt)
  - **LCR (Liquidity Coverage Ratio):** Banks must hold enough liquid assets for 30-day stress
  - **NSFR (Net Stable Funding Ratio):** Long-term stable funding requirement

## Pillar 3: Current Affairs — The Daily Habit

### Why Current Affairs is a Daily Habit, Not a Pre-Exam Sprint

Banking exams ask current affairs from the **last 6–12 months**. If you try to cover 12 months in the last 30 days before an exam, you are trying to memorize 3,000+ facts in 30 days — nearly impossible.

But if you read 5–10 news items every day for 9 months, you naturally absorb and retain the same 3,000 facts without stress.

**Rule:** Start current affairs reading from **Month 2 (September 2026)** and never stop.

### What to Read Daily (Pick 1–2 Sources Only)

| Source | Time | Best For |
|--------|------|---------|
| **Inshorts App** | 10 min | Quick 5-sentence summaries of top news |
| **BankersAdda Daily Quiz** | 10 min | Banking + current affairs questions |
| **PIB (pib.gov.in)** | 15 min | Government press releases (official) |
| **Hindu/Financial Express** | 20–30 min | In-depth news + editorials |
| **Oliveboard/Testbook Monthly Capsule** | 60 min (monthly) | Comprehensive monthly revision |

> **Recommendation:** Inshorts (daily) + Oliveboard Monthly Capsule (monthly revision) = Efficient and comprehensive

### What to Note in Your CA Notebook

Every day, note only items that fall in these categories:

**Category 1 — RBI & Banking News**
- Policy rate changes (Repo rate, CRR, SLR)
- New banking circulars and guidelines
- New digital payment systems or updates
- Bank mergers, acquisitions, new bank licenses

**Category 2 — Government Schemes**
- New schemes launched (Minister, purpose, beneficiaries)
- Budget allocations and major announcements
- Changes to existing schemes (eligibility, benefit amount)

**Category 3 — Appointments**
- New RBI Governor or Deputy Governors
- New Finance Secretary, Economic Affairs Secretary
- New bank CEOs/MDs
- New heads of SEBI, IRDA, PFRDA
- New Union Cabinet Ministers

**Category 4 — International**
- Summit meetings (G20, BRICS, SCO, ASEAN, Commonwealth)
- India's bilateral agreements (with which country, for what purpose)
- New IMF/World Bank reports and India's rankings

**Category 5 — Sports, Awards & Records**
- Major sports championships: Winner, country
- Nobel Prize winners (announce October every year)
- Oscar/Grammy if particularly prominent
- Padma Awards (announced January every year)

**Category 6 — Economy**
- GDP growth data (quarterly)
- Inflation data (CPI/WPI — monthly)
- India's rankings in global indices (Ease of Doing Business, HDI, GHI, etc.)
- Trade data, forex reserves

### Current Affairs Revision System

| Frequency | Activity |
|-----------|---------|
| **Daily** | Read + note 5–10 items in CA notebook |
| **Weekly (Sunday)** | Review all notes from that week (10–15 min) |
| **Monthly** | Solve monthly CA quiz from Oliveboard/Testbook (50 questions) |
| **Every 3 months** | Comprehensive recap of last 3 months' CA |
| **Phase 4 (Apr–May)** | Full 12-month CA revision using monthly capsules |
